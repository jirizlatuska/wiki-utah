# Smoky Mountain Road & Escalante / Boulder

**Summary**: Remote BLM road from Big Water north across the Kaiparowits Plateau to Escalante; then UT-12 east to Boulder. Planned as the transition between Coyote Buttes and Goblin Valley (~Aug 2).

**Sources**: User planning notes.

**Last updated**: 2026-05-18

---

## Route Overview

**Stateline Campground → Big Water → Smoky Mountain Road → Escalante → Boulder**

| Segment | Distance | Surface | Notes |
|---------|----------|---------|-------|
| Stateline → Big Water (US-89) | ~60 mi | Paved | ~1 hr |
| Smoky Mountain Road (Big Water → Escalante) | ~78 mi | Dirt/gravel | 4–6 hrs; high clearance; see below |
| Escalante → Boulder (UT-12) | ~26 mi | Paved | 45 min; extremely scenic |

Total driving: roughly 7–8 hours on a single day — plan an early start.

---

## Smoky Mountain Road

- Starts at Big Water, UT (US-89, ~15 miles east of Kanab / ~15 miles west of Page)
- Runs north ~78 miles across the Kaiparowits Plateau to Escalante
- High clearance recommended; 4WD useful in wet sections
- Named for historic underground coal seam fires that produced visible smoke
- Very remote — no services, no cell coverage; carry extra water and fuel
- **Monsoon risk**: clay surface becomes extremely slick when wet; impassable after heavy rain — same caution as Cottonwood Canyon Road. Check conditions before committing.

**BLM 300**: User notes this road number in connection with this route — likely a BLM designation for part of Smoky Mountain Road or a connecting spur. *(Needs verification against BLM GSENM maps.)*

### What You See
- Kaiparowits Plateau: vast remote tableland with coal-bearing strata
- Wide canyon and mesa views with virtually no other traffic
- The "smoky mountain" area where coal veins have historically smoldered

---

## Escalante

- Small town; last services before Boulder or the backcountry
- **Escalante Interagency Visitor Center**: GSENM maps and current road conditions; worth a stop to verify Smoky Mountain Road passability before driving it
- **Devil's Garden**: off Hole-in-the-Rock Road ~12 miles south of town; surreal cluster of hoodoos and arches; short easy walk; excellent for a child

---

## Boulder

- **Anasazi State Park Museum**: collection of Ancestral Puebloan artifacts and a partially excavated village site (~1050–1200 AD); well-curated, good for a child; small admission fee
- **Burr Trail Road** starts here — paved initially, then unpaved into Capitol Reef/Waterpocket Fold; connects eventually to Goblin Valley direction

---

## Calf Creek Recreation Area (between Escalante and Boulder on UT-12)

- **Lower Calf Creek Falls**: 126-ft waterfall at the end of a 5.5-mile round-trip canyon hike; sandy trail along a creek; one of the best hikes in the region; very doable for a child
- BLM day-use fee; small campground on-site (first come, first served)

---

## Camping Options (Aug 2)

- **Calf Creek Campground** (BLM, UT-12): small, basic, beautiful — best option if available; no reservations
- **Escalante Petrified Forest State Park** (near Escalante): state park campground with reservoir; reservable
- **Dispersed camping** on BLM land east/south of Escalante along Hole-in-the-Rock Road

---

## Routing to Goblin Valley (Aug 3)

Boulder → UT-12 east (Escalante to Torrey, ~70 mi, paved, stunning) → Torrey → UT-24 east → Goblin Valley Road → Goblin Valley SP

Drive time: ~2.5 hours; leaves the morning relaxed.
