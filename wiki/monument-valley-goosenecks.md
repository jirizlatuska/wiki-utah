# Monument Valley & Goosenecks

**Summary**: Monument Valley Navajo Tribal Park and Goosenecks State Park on the Utah/Arizona border — iconic red-rock buttes and entrenched river meanders.

**Sources**: User planning notes; takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip

**Last updated**: 2026-05-18

---

## Monument Valley Navajo Tribal Park

- On the UT/AZ border; administered by the Navajo Nation (not NPS)
- Entrance fee required; Navajo Nation hours apply
- **Valley Drive**: 17-mile unpaved loop through the mittens and buttes — fine for SUV; allow 2–3 hours
- **The View Campground**: inside the park, right at the rim — best camping option here; reservable in advance; fills fast in summer
- Guided jeep tours available if you want to access restricted areas (Mystery Valley, etc.)
- Already in user's saved Google Maps places (source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)

### July notes
- Extremely hot; sunrise/sunset light is dramatically better than midday anyway
- Monsoon storms in the afternoon can produce spectacular light over the buttes

## Goosenecks State Park

- 10 miles west of Mexican Hat, UT on UT-316
- Free overlook (day use fee minimal) over the deeply entrenched meanders of the San Juan River — 1,000 ft below
- One of the best geological viewpoints in the Southwest
- Primitive camping available on-site (first come, first served; very basic)
- Already in user's saved Google Maps places (source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)

## Routing

Monument Valley → Goosenecks: north on US-163 to Mexican Hat (~30 min), then UT-316 west (~10 min)
Goosenecks → Page: US-163 to US-89 south (~1.5 hrs)
Goosenecks → Kanab: US-163 to US-163/US-191 north, then UT-261 (Moki Dugway) to UT-95 to US-89 north (~3 hrs scenic) — or faster via US-163 → US-89
