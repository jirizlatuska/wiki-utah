## Overview

Goblin Valley State Park protects a basin filled with thousands of eroded Entrada sandstone formations (called "goblins" or hoodoos). Completely open to roam — no trails required, you walk among the goblins freely. Excellent for a child.

- Located near Hanksville, UT (UT-24 west of Green River)
- Planned dates: **August 3–4**
- Already in user's saved Google Maps (source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)

## Camping

- **Goblin Valley State Park Campground**: right at the park entrance; shade shelters, flush toilets, showers; reservable through Utah State Parks
- Books up — reserve in advance, especially for summer weekends
- Alternative: dispersed camping on BLM land along Goblin Valley Road (outside the state park)

---

## What to Do

- **Valley of Goblins**: free roaming among the formations — allow 2–3 hours; very kid-friendly
- **Carmel Canyon Loop**: 1.5-mile trail with views over the valley
- **Wild Horse Canyon**: slot canyon on adjacent BLM land, short drive from the park
- **Little Wild Horse Canyon**: nearby slot canyon (San Rafael Swell); moderate, good for older kids; can be combined with Bell Canyon as a loop

## Routing Context

- From Stateline Campground (Coyote Buttes): ~3.5 hrs via US-89 north → US-89/UT-9 → UT-24 north → Goblin Valley Road
  - Or via Kanab → US-89 → UT-12 → UT-24 (more scenic, similar time)
- From Goblin Valley onward toward Capitol Reef / Canyonlands: well-positioned

---

## Nearby (San Rafael Swell)

- **Little Wild Horse Canyon**: 8-mile loop with Bell Canyon; accessible for kids; starts ~10 miles from Goblin Valley
- **Wedge Overlook**: dramatic canyon of the San Rafael River; 20 miles north; already in user's saved Google Maps
- **San Rafael Reef**: long wall of upturned rock visible from UT-24
