# Capitol Reef — Cathedral Valley & Muley Twist

**Summary**: The remote northern (Cathedral Valley) and southern (Waterpocket Fold / Upper Muley Twist) districts of Capitol Reef National Park, plus the Moonscape Overlook and Caineville Badlands on UT-24.

## Moonscape Overlook & Caineville Badlands

- Along UT-24 between Hanksville and Capitol Reef Visitor Center
- "Moonscape" refers to the barren, multicolored bentonite badlands of the Caineville area — striking from the road and from several pullouts
- **Factory Butte**: prominent flat-topped butte visible from UT-24; BLM land, dispersed OHV area; dramatic silhouette
- Several informal overlook pullouts; no fee, no facilities

## Cathedral Valley (Northern Capitol Reef)

Remote northern district of Capitol Reef NP — some of the most otherworldly scenery in Utah.

### Access
Two main approaches; most people do a one-way loop:
- **East entry (Hartnet Road)**: from UT-24, ~20 miles east of the Capitol Reef Visitor Center; includes **Hartnet Ford** (water crossing of Fremont River — check water level before committing; avoidable via Cathedral Road if high)
- **West entry (Cathedral Road / Caineville Wash Road)**: from UT-24 near Caineville, ~11 miles west of Hanksville
- Full loop ~58 miles of unpaved road; allow a full day

### Key Features
- **Temple of the Sun**: ~400 ft sandstone monolith rising from the valley floor
- **Temple of the Moon**: slightly smaller companion monolith
- **Glass Mountain**: large gypsum deposit; appears white and crystalline
- **The Walls / Wall of Jericho**: long sandstone escarpment
- **Bentonite Hills**: colorful banded clay hills near the south end of the loop
- **Upper Cathedral Valley Overlook**: panoramic view over the whole valley — already in user's saved Google Maps (×3 entries, source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)

### Notes
- High clearance required throughout; 4WD recommended
- No facilities; no water; carry extra fuel (no gas between Hanksville and Torrey)
- Hartnet Ford: check current depth — impassable when flooded; monsoon rain upstream raises it quickly

## Upper Muley Twist Canyon

- Southern Capitol Reef NP; Waterpocket Fold / Strike Valley area
- **Access**: Burr Trail Road east from Boulder → unpaved section → Strike Valley trailhead
- **Upper canyon**: ~15 miles round trip to complete the full upper canyon and Strike Valley Overlook; shorter out-and-back options available
- Narrow canyon cutting through the Waterpocket Fold; arches, potholes, sculpted sandstone
- Already in user's saved Google Maps (source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)
- Morning start recommended (flash flood risk in the narrows)

### Strike Valley Overlook
- 3-mile round trip from the Burr Trail trailhead
- Panoramic view over the entire Waterpocket Fold; one of the best in Capitol Reef

## Routing Through This Area (after Goblin Valley, ~Aug 5–7)

**Logical sequence:**

1. Goblin Valley → UT-24 west → Moonscape/Caineville Badlands (Factory Butte, roadside views)
2. Continue west on UT-24 → Hartnet Road north into Cathedral Valley (full day loop)
3. Return to UT-24; head toward Notom-Bullfrog Road south
4. Or: Torrey → UT-12 → Boulder → Burr Trail east → Upper Muley Twist
   (Henry Mountains work as a side trip from either Hanksville or Notom-Bullfrog Road)

## Camping

- **Cathedral Valley Campground** (Capitol Reef NP): 6 primitive sites inside the valley; first come, first served; no water; outstanding location — already in user's saved Google Maps
- **Cedar Mesa Campground** (Capitol Reef NP, Notom-Bullfrog Road): 5 sites near the Waterpocket Fold; primitive
- Dispersed BLM camping throughout the area
