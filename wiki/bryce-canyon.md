# Bryce Canyon (Option A fork)

**Summary**: Bryce Canyon National Park — hoodoo amphitheaters at high elevation. One of two options after Capitol Reef, depending on weather/mood. Camping at Kodachrome Basin State Park.

**Sources**: User planning notes; takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip

**Last updated**: 2026-05-18

---

## Overview

Bryce Canyon sits at 8,000–9,115 ft — noticeably cooler than the desert floor. A major advantage in August heat. The park is a series of amphitheaters eroded into the Pink Cliffs, filled with thousands of hoodoos.

---

## Key Viewpoints & Hikes

| Stop | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Sunrise Point / Sunset Point | Best rim overlooks; iconic hoodoo panoramas |
| Navajo Loop Trail | 1.4 miles; descends into the amphitheater; very doable for a child |
| Queens Garden Trail | 1.8 miles; gentler descent; connects to Navajo Loop |
| Inspiration Point | Best overview of the Silent City hoodoo forest |
| Rainbow Point | Southern end of the scenic drive; highest point in the park |
| Fairyland Loop | 8 miles; less crowded; more remote feel |

The Navajo Loop + Queens Garden combination (~3 miles) is the classic circuit and well-suited for a child.

## Camping

**Kodachrome Basin State Park** (~25 miles east of Bryce via UT-12 and Cottonwood Canyon Road or via Cannonville):
- Already in user's saved Google Maps (source: takeout-20260518T231412Z-3-001.zip)
- Colorful spires and sand pipes; good campground with facilities
- Reservable through Utah State Parks
- Note: if coming via Cottonwood Canyon Road from the south, same monsoon/wet-road caveat applies

Bryce Canyon NP campgrounds (North Campground, Sunset Campground):
- Inside the park; closer to the rim
- Reserve well in advance on recreation.gov — August fills up
