# Henry Mountains

**Summary**: Remote laccolith mountain range south of Hanksville, UT — includes Mount Pennell and a free-roaming bison herd. Accessed via Bull Creek Pass Road.

**Sources**: User planning notes.

**Last updated**: 2026-05-18

---

## Overview

The Henry Mountains are one of the last-named mountain ranges in the contiguous US — so remote they weren't mapped until 1869. Five main peaks, all laccoliths (igneous intrusions that domed the overlying sedimentary rock). Surrounded by canyon country on all sides.

| Peak | Elevation |
|------|-----------|
| Mount Ellen | 11,522 ft |
| **Mount Pennell** | **11,371 ft** |
| Mount Hillers | 10,723 ft |
| Mount Holmes | 7,930 ft |
| Mount Ellsworth | 8,235 ft |

---

## Mount Pennell

- Second-highest peak; central massif of the range
- **Bull Creek Pass Road** (high clearance, 4WD recommended) traverses the range between Mount Ellen and Mount Pennell; dramatic alpine views over the surrounding desert
- Mount Pennell summit: scramble/hike from Bull Creek Pass area; no maintained trail; route-finding required
- **Views**: San Rafael Desert, Canyonlands, Lake Powell, Capitol Reef — exceptional 360° panorama

### Access
- From Hanksville: south on UT-95 briefly, then turn onto Henry Mountains Road (BLM); follow signs for Bull Creek Pass
- From Notom-Bullfrog Road: turnoff west into the range
- Allow a full day; roads are slow

---

## Bison Herd

- A herd of ~300–500 American bison roams the Henry Mountains — one of the few free-ranging, publicly owned herds in the US, descended from animals brought in 1941
- Commonly seen along Bull Creek Pass Road and the lower slopes
- Excellent for a child; bison are large and photogenic; keep distance (BLM guidance: 75+ feet minimum)

---

## Camping

- Dispersed BLM camping throughout the range; no facilities
- **McMillan Springs Campground** (BLM): near Bull Creek Pass area; basic, free; high elevation (~10,000 ft) — noticeably cooler than the desert floor in July

---

## Notes

- Carry extra fuel: no services between Hanksville and Bullfrog Marina (~75 miles)
- High elevation means afternoon thunderstorms in July — common and intense; plan to be off exposed ridges by noon
- Bull Creek Pass Road: snow possible even in July at the highest point (rare but not unknown); check conditions

---

## Routing Context

- Natural pair with Cathedral Valley: do the Henrys the day after Cathedral Valley while based near Hanksville
- Or combine with Notom-Bullfrog Road drive south toward Burr Trail / Muley Twist
